---
title: Successive halving
description: Screen many candidates under a fixed budget, killing losers early.
---

With many candidate hypotheses or configurations and a fixed compute budget, running each to completion is wasteful. **Successive halving** gives every candidate a small budget, keeps the top fraction, multiplies the budget, and repeats — a tournament of experiments that spends compute on survivors.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | A budget-doubling tournament that culls the weakest candidates each rung. |
| **When** | Many candidates, a fixed budget, and you want the best without running each to completion. |
| **In → out** | Candidates and a way to evaluate them at a budget → the winner and the elimination trace. |

## At each rung

```text
rung r:  evaluate survivors at budget  b · η^r
         keep the top  1/η
         repeat
```

Each rung `r` multiplies the per-candidate budget by `η` and culls to the best `1/η`. Losers die early and cheaply; the winner emerges having earned the most compute.

## Example (η = 3)

```text
rung 0:  27 candidates ·  budget b     → keep 9
rung 1:   9 candidates ·  budget 3b    → keep 3
rung 2:   3 candidates ·  budget 9b    → keep 1  (winner)
```

**Hyperband** wraps this by trying several starting budgets, so it isn't over-committed to one early-versus-late trade-off.
