---
title: Bayesian optimization
description: Expensive, continuous experiment selection via a Gaussian-process surrogate and Expected Improvement.
---

When each experiment is expensive (a long sandbox run, a costly simulation) and the knob being tuned is **continuous** — a threshold, a rate, a hyperparameter — discrete options don't fit. Bayesian optimization models the objective with a **Gaussian-process surrogate** and proposes the next input that maximises an acquisition function. This is formal **optimal experimental design**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | A GP surrogate over the objective plus an acquisition function that proposes the next point. |
| **When** | Each experiment is expensive and the input is continuous. |
| **In → out** | Observed `(x, y)` points and input bounds → the next `x` to evaluate. |

## Acquisition — Expected Improvement

Under the GP posterior, score each candidate `x` by how much it's expected to beat the best result so far:

```text
EI(x) = E[ max(0, f(x) − f_best) ]
```

Then evaluate the `x` that scores highest. Expected Improvement automatically balances exploration (high-variance regions) against exploitation (high-mean regions) — no explicit exploration term to tune.

## How a step works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fit the surrogate">Fit a Gaussian process to the observed points.</Step>
  <Step title="Score the space">Compute Expected Improvement across the bounded input space under the posterior.</Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate the best candidate">Run the experiment at the highest-scoring input, and append the new result.</Step>
  <Step title="Repeat">Continue until the budget is spent or improvement stalls.</Step>
</Steps>
