---
title: Framing hypotheses
description: How Reagent holds several competing, testable hypotheses at once — and lets evidence decide between them.
---

One hypothesis is advocacy, not research. Reagent turns the literature into **at least three rival hypotheses that genuinely disagree**, then makes them compete. Holding several explanations at once — mechanism A vs. mechanism B vs. "no effect / a confound" — is what lets evidence, rather than preference, decide.

## What makes a good hypothesis here

Each rival is stated so it can be tested, and carries three things:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="A null hypothesis" icon="minus-circle">
    The default to beat — what you'd believe if the effect isn't real.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What would confirm it" icon="check-circle">
    The observation that would count as support.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What would refute it" icon="x-circle">
    The crucial test whose outcome would kill it. This is the most important part.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Rivals should span the space of explanations, not be three phrasings of one idea. Reagent merges near-duplicates so compute is spent on distinct explanations, and a critique pass kills anything logically broken *before* an experiment is wasted on it.

## The lifecycle of a hypothesis

A hypothesis is durable — it lives in the workspace, not the conversation, so it survives reloads and separate sessions. As evidence arrives, its status moves:

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Alive** | In play, still competing. |
| **Supported** | Survived its crucial test. |
| **Refuted** | The evidence killed it — a result, not a failure. |
| **Merged** | Folded into a near-identical rival. |
| **Evolved** | Bred into a sharper successor. |
