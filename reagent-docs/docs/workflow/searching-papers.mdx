Before it hypothesizes or analyses anything, Reagent grounds itself in real papers — it prevents reinventing the wheel and keeps every claim traceable. Rather than one search box, it can reach **42 scientific sources** spanning the literature, life sciences, and chemistry, and picks the right ones per topic.

## The sources

| Domain | Count | Examples |
|--------|:-----:|----------|
| Genomics | 14 | Ensembl, NCBI, MyGene, UCSC, MyVariant |
| Literature | 7 | OpenAlex, PubMed, Europe PMC, arXiv, Semantic Scholar |
| Proteomics | 7 | UniProt, IntAct |
| Chemistry | 6 | PubChem, ChEMBL |
| Structure | 4 | RCSB PDB |
| Biology | 3 | — |
| Clinical | 1 | — |

Reagent works across **any field** — it chooses the broadest general index for a fresh topic, and the domain-specific sources when the question is biomedical, chemical, or structural.

## How a search runs

<Steps>
  <Step title="Decompose the topic into facets">
    A broad question is split into 3–5 distinct facets, each searched independently so coverage is wide, not shallow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cast a wide net, in parallel">
    Each facet runs several varied queries — original terms, synonyms, broader concepts, and specific method or dataset names — across multiple databases at once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deduplicate and prefer the best version">
    Results are merged and de-duplicated by DOI and title, preferring the peer-reviewed version over a preprint.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read, don't skim">
    For the papers that matter, Reagent reads the full text and extracts the actual claims, methods, and numbers — not a summary from the title.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Papers worth keeping are added to your workspace **library**, so a research accumulates its own corpus you can revisit and review.

<Panel title="Real papers only">
  Every paper Reagent cites is one it actually retrieved and read, with its authors, year, venue, and DOI verified. If it can't find a real source for a claim, it says so — it never invents a citation.
</Panel>
