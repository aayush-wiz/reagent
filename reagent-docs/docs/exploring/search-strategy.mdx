Reagent rarely can test every hypothesis. Exploration is a **search over a tree**: a node is a hypothesis (or an experiment plan), expanding it generates its children (refinements, follow-up experiments), and each node has a score `h(node)`.

```text
h(node) = tournament rating · evidential support · expected information gain
```

For hypotheses the score is the [Elo](/ranking/elo) rating from the tournament plus evidential support; for experiments it's the expected information gain.

## The strategies

| Strategy | Behaviour | Use when |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| **BFS** | Level-by-level, all depth-1 before depth-2. | Shallow space; you want coverage. |
| **DFS** | Down one branch before backtracking. | A strong lead worth chasing; backtracking is cheap. |
| **Best-first** *(default)* | Always expand the highest-scoring node. | You can score nodes — the tournament gives a rating. |
| **Beam (width k)** | Keep only the top-`k` each level; prune the rest. | Wide branching, bounded cost. `k = 3–5` rivals. |
| **MCTS** | Balance explore/exploit via UCB over rollouts. | Sequential, multi-stage experimental programs. |

## Exploration vs exploitation

Every strategy trades **exploiting** the current best node against **exploring** uncertain ones.

- Pure exploitation (greedy on the leader) risks a local optimum — you confirm a good-looking wrong hypothesis.
- Pure exploration (try everything) wastes budget.
- **Best-first or beam scored by the tournament, plus a bandit rule for *which experiment next*, is the balanced default.**

## Keeping the tournament cheap

Comparing every pair of hypotheses grows as **O(n²)**. For a large board, Reagent bounds it:

- **Beam** — only compare the top-`k` rivals against each other.
- **Swiss pairing** — each round, pair hypotheses of similar rating; a few rounds rank a big field cheaply.
- **Successive halving** — give every hypothesis a small test, keep the top half, repeat ([Breeding](/breeding/successive-halving)).

## The practical default

Beam search, width **3–5** over the hypothesis board, scored by the tournament, expanding the leader and its nearest rival each cycle, and choosing the next experiment with a bandit or [Bayesian-optimization](/selection/bayesian-optimization) rule when experiments are expensive. Beam width is simply how many rivals you carry. Escalate to MCTS only for genuinely multi-stage programs.
